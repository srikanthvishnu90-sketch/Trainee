New findings in this pass:
- **A heading could pass as a fact. Fixed in the product's guard (architect, second round).** An SEC filing's heading ("Form 10-K Hollow Oak Logistics Inc.") was kept as a fact: "10-K" read as a figure and the words are on the page. The market facts guard now reads a form code as a name, not a number (figuresIn), and a fact needs a predicate beyond names: at least one lowercase content word that is not in its page's title (hasPredicate).
- **Fixture facts read as fixtures.** Facts converted from JSON keep their source's own words ("Silverpine Robotics (Wikidata Q99000101): ..."), which are accurate but stiff.
- **Sources that disagree. Fixed (architect, second round).** When two cited sources give different figures for the same thing (the same measure, attached to that measure's words, about the same names), the brief keeps both facts and adds "Where the sources differ", with each value and its source and the line "The sources differ; Trainee has not picked one." An inline answer is told to give both. F-RE-093 shows it: headcount 600 [1] (the company's page) and 3,900 [3] (its 10-K). Detection is narrow on purpose; differences it does not recognize (other wordings, other measures) are not flagged.
